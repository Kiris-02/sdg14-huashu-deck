The Atmospheric Absorption Driver

The global ocean has absorbed approximately 25–30% of anthropogenic CO₂ emissions since the industrial revolution, altering marine carbonate equilibrium on a planetary scale.

Scientific Cooperation Is the Target

Unlike Targets 14.1 and 14.2, no single coastal sovereign can isolate or fix its chemical regime alone. Scientific observation and data exchange are formally built into the target mandate.

Accurate Terminology: Becoming Less Alkaline

The ocean is not turning acidic (pH < 7). Rather, mean surface seawater pH has dropped from ~8.2 to ~8.1—representing a ~30% increase in hydrogen ion concentration.

Ocean Carbonate System Cascade Biogeochemical Chain
CO₂ + H₂O
1. Gas Dissolution & Acid Formation

Atmospheric carbon dioxide dissolves in surface seawater to form carbonic acid (H₂CO₃).

H₂CO₃ → H⁺ + HCO₃⁻
2. Hydrogen Ion Release (pH Drop)

Carbonic acid rapidly dissociates into hydrogen ions (H⁺) and bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻), lowering seawater pH.

H⁺ + CO₃²⁻ → HCO₃⁻
3. Carbonate Ion Depletion

Free H⁺ ions bond with ambient carbonate ions (CO₃²⁻), stripping the critical building block needed for shell formation.

⚠
Biological Calcification Crisis: Reduced saturation states of aragonite and calcite make it energetically difficult for pteropods, shellfish, and coral reefs to construct and maintain their shells and skeletons.
Source: UN Statistics Division, Target 14.3; IOC-UNESCO Ocean Acidification Programme [1, 9–10].
Rule: Note that the ocean is becoming less alkaline, not turning chemically acidic (pH remains > 7.0).
